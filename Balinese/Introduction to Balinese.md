# 🌺 Introduction to Balinese

**Target variety:** everyday Balinese (**Basa Bali**) used in Bali, Indonesia, with beginner-friendly polite/neutral forms.

## 🌍 Where is Balinese spoken?

Balinese is mainly spoken on the Indonesian island of Bali. Balinese communities also live in other parts of Indonesia, including Lombok and Java. Most Balinese speakers also know Indonesian, the national language.

Depending on the source and year, estimates vary, but Balinese has several million speakers.

## 📜 A Little History

Balinese belongs to the **Austronesian** language family. Its history is closely connected with Java and the wider Indonesian archipelago. Old Javanese (Kawi) and Sanskrit have influenced Balinese vocabulary, literature, religion and ceremonies.

Balinese developed its own rich traditions while remaining connected to neighbouring languages.

## 🗣️ What Makes Balinese Special?

One important feature is its **speech levels**. People may choose different words depending on who they are speaking to and how respectful the situation needs to be.

Balinese also has a traditional writing system called **Aksara Bali**. Roman letters are commonly used for modern learning, signs and communication, so this course uses Roman spelling first.

Balinese can express time without changing verbs in exactly the same way English does. You will learn useful patterns instead of complicated grammar rules.

## 🎵 Language and Culture

Balinese is closely connected with:

- 🎶 Gamelan music
- 💃 Traditional dance
- 🛕 Temple ceremonies
- 🍚 Balinese food
- 📖 Storytelling and literature
- 👨‍👩‍👧 Family and community life

In tourist areas, you will often hear Indonesian and English too.

## ✨ 5 Fun Facts

1. Balinese has its own traditional script.
2. Balinese has several speech levels.
3. Sanskrit and Old Javanese have influenced its vocabulary.
4. Bali is famous for gamelan music and dance traditions.
5. Many Balinese people speak more than one language.

## 🌎 Language Around the World

Outside Bali, Balinese can be heard in Balinese communities elsewhere in Indonesia and among Balinese people living overseas.

## ❤️ Why Learn Balinese?

Even a few words can help you connect with people and show respect for Bali's culture. You can greet someone, thank them, order food and have tiny conversations.

| I / me | tiang | tee-AHNG | 'tee' + 'ahng' |
| you | ragane | rah-GAH-neh | 'rah' + 'gah' + 'neh' |
| thank you | suksma | SOOKS-mah | 'sooks' + 'mah' |
| sorry / excuse me | ampura | ahm-POO-rah | 'am' + 'poo' + 'rah' |
| good | becik | beh-CHICK | 'be' + 'chick' |
| yes / okay | nggih | NGEEH | soft 'ng' start |
| no / not | sing | SING | like English 'sing' |
| here | dini | DEE-nee | 'dee' + 'nee' |
| there | ditu | DEE-too | 'dee' + 'too' |
| what | napi | NAH-pee | 'nah' + 'pee' |


### Tiny conversation

**A:** Suksma. — *SOOKS-mah.* — Thank you.  
**B:** Nggih. — *NGEEH.* — Yes / okay.  
**A:** Ampura. — *ahm-POO-rah.* — Sorry / excuse me.  
**B:** Sing kenken. — *sing ken-KEN.* — It's okay / no worries.

> 🌺 **Culture tip:** **Om Swastiastu** is an important traditional Balinese greeting, especially in ceremonial and formal contexts. Learn it respectfully rather than using it as a casual replacement for every “hello”.

### 📚 Grown-up note

Balinese speech levels and pronouns can be complex. This workbook deliberately teaches a small, useful beginner set rather than pretending there is one form that works in every social situation.
